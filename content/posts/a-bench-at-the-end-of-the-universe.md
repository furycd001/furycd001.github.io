Sometimes I wonder whether the universe really goes on forever, or whether, somewhere out there, beyond everything we have ever seen or could ever hope to understand, there might be an end.

We tend to imagine the universe as something infinite, an endless expanse of stars, galaxies and darkness stretching out in every direction. But what if it isn't? What if the universe is more like a road, a coastline or a vast piece of land? What if, somewhere beyond all that we know, there is a final boundary where everything simply stops?

I find something strangely beautiful about that thought, and I wonder whether you might too.

Imagine travelling through the universe, passing distant galaxies, watching stars form and die, drifting through vast stretches of space where the light from distant stars takes millions of years to reach you. Imagine the unimaginable distances, the countless things we would see along the way, and the sheer amount of time it would take to reach the very end.

And then, one day, we arrive.

There is no grand entrance, no sign announcing that we have reached our destination. Perhaps there is simply a boundary, a final edge beyond which there is no more universe. No stars, no galaxies, no space as we understand it. Just an incomprehensible nothingness stretching out beyond the place where everything ends.

And there, waiting for us, is a bench.

An ordinary bench.

We sit down and look out into the nothingness. After an eternity of travelling through the greatest expanse imaginable, we have finally reached the end. There is nowhere else to go, nothing left to discover beyond that final boundary. For the first time, the journey is over.

Or perhaps we turn around.

Perhaps the most extraordinary thing about reaching the end isn't the nothingness in front of us, but the universe behind us. All those galaxies, all those stars, all those worlds and all those billions of years that somehow led us to this one quiet place. From the bench, we look back across the unimaginable distance we have travelled, and for a moment, we see the universe not as an endless mystery, but as a journey with a beginning, a middle and an end.

And perhaps, after travelling so far to reach the edge of everything, we would begin to wonder whether the greatest wonder lies in finally reaching the end, or in looking back at the extraordinary journey that brought us there.

Of course, this is only a thought experiment. Modern cosmology doesn't establish that the universe has a physical edge like the end of a road. Space could be infinite, or it could be finite without having a boundary in the ordinary sense. Our understanding of what lies beyond the observable universe is still limited, and the idea of a literal edge remains speculative.

But perhaps that is part of the appeal. We can imagine possibilities that we may never be able to experience, let alone prove. We can look up at the night sky, see only a tiny fraction of what exists, and still find ourselves wondering what might lie beyond everything we know.

And I keep returning to that bench.

Maybe there is no bench, no edge and no final destination. Maybe the universe continues forever, and every possible ending is simply another mystery waiting to be explored. But there is something comforting about imagining that, somewhere in the grandest journey imaginable, there could be a place for us to stop, sit down and take it all in.

Because even if the universe has no end, perhaps we can still find something meaningful in the idea of one.

A quiet place to rest. A chance to look back. A moment to appreciate the distance we have travelled, rather than worrying about how much further there is to go.

And if there really were a bench at the end of the universe, what would we do when we finally reached it?

Would we sit facing the nothingness, trying to comprehend what it means for everything to stop? Or would we turn around, look back at the universe behind us, and realise that the most extraordinary part was never reaching the end at all?

Perhaps we would simply sit there for a while, together in the silence, looking out at the impossible.

And maybe, for the first time in an eternity, we wouldn't feel the need to go anywhere else.
